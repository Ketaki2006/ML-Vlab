1. Title of Experiment

Dimensionality Reduction: Implementation and Comparative Analysis of Linear (PCA, SVD) and Non-Linear (t-SNE, UMAP) Techniques on High-Dimensional Clinical Biomedical Data.

2. Aim

To implement, analyze, and comparatively evaluate linear dimensionality reduction methods (Principal Component Analysis, Singular Value Decomposition) and non-linear manifold learning algorithms (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection) on the 30-dimensional Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer dataset, evaluating their computational efficiency, variance retention, topological structure preservation, and clinical cluster discriminability.

3. Objectives

  1. To ingest, validate, and standardize the 30 real-valued pathological features of the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer dataset.
  2. To implement exact mathematical formulations of PCA via sample covariance matrix eigendecomposition and SVD via Gram matrix factorization.
  3. To implement and configure non-linear manifold embeddings (t-SNE and UMAP) with hyperparameter exploration (perplexity, iterations, number of neighbors, minimum distance).
  4. To project 30-dimensional biopsy data into 2D and 3D coordinate subspaces and assess visual separability of Benign vs. Malignant tumor classes.
  5. To quantitatively record real algorithm execution times, explained variance ratios, singular values, and Fisher cluster separability scores to draw rigorous scientific conclusions.

4. Theoretical Background

In biomedical pattern recognition, working in high-dimensional feature spaces ($\mathbb{R}^D$, where $D=30$) introduces the Curse of Dimensionality. As dimensionality increases, the volume of the space grows exponentially, causing samples to become exponentially sparse. Furthermore, the distance concentration phenomenon dictates that the Euclidean distance between arbitrary pairs of points converges to a near-constant value:

$$\lim_{D \to \infty} \frac{\text{dist}_{\max} - \text{dist}_{\min}}{\text{dist}_{\min}} = 0$$

This degradation reduces the efficacy of distance-based classifiers and clustering algorithms. Dimensionality reduction seeks an optimal mapping function $f: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \in \{2, 3\}$) that compresses redundant collinear features while retaining the underlying intrinsic geometry or global variance of the data.

5. Dataset Description

The experiment utilizes the Breast Cancer Wisconsin (Diagnostic) Dataset (WDBC) from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, generated by Dr. William H. Wolberg, W. Nick Street, and Olvi L. Mangasarian (University of Wisconsin):

  • Total Samples ($N$): 569 patient fine-needle aspirate (FNA) biopsy samples.
  • Attribute Count ($D$): 30 continuous numerical features computed from digitized images of cell nuclei.
  • Class Distribution: 357 Benign ($62.7\%$) and 212 Malignant ($37.3\%$) instances.
  • Feature Composition: Mean, Standard Error (SE), and "Worst" (mean of the three largest values) computed for ten fundamental morphological characteristics: radius, texture, perimeter, area, smoothness, compactness, concavity, concave points, symmetry, and fractal dimension.

6. Algorithms Used

  1. Principal Component Analysis (PCA): Unsupervised linear orthogonal transformation that projects data onto directions of maximal sample variance.
  2. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD / Truncated SVD): Factorizes the data matrix into orthogonal left/right singular vectors and singular values, producing optimal low-rank matrix approximations.
  3. t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE): Probabilistic non-linear manifold learning algorithm that preserves local neighborhood affinities using Gaussian distributions in high-dimensional space and Student-t distributions in low-dimensional space.
  4. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP): Manifold learning technique founded on Riemannian geometry and algebraic topology, modeling data as fuzzy simplicial sets to balance local cluster cohesion and global trajectory preservation.

7. Mathematical Formulations

A. Data Standardization (Z-Score)

$$z_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}, \quad \text{where } \mu_j = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_{ij}, \quad \sigma_j = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N (x_{ij} - \mu_j)^2}$$

B. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

$$\mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{N-1} \mathbf{Z}^T \mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}, \quad \mathbf{C} \mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i \mathbf{v}_i, \quad \text{EVR}_i = \frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_{j=1}^D \lambda_j} \times 100\%$$ $$\mathbf{Y}_{\text{PCA}} = \mathbf{Z} \mathbf{W}_k, \quad \text{where } \mathbf{W}_k = [\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_k] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times k}$$

C. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)

$$\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T, \quad \mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Sigma}^2 \mathbf{V}^T, \quad \sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})}$$ $$\mathbf{Y}_{\text{SVD}} = \mathbf{X} \mathbf{V}_k = \mathbf{U}_k \mathbf{\Sigma}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times k}$$

D. t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE)

$$p_{j|i} = \frac{\exp(-\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|^2 / 2\sigma_i^2)}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp(-\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_k\|^2 / 2\sigma_i^2)}, \quad p_{ij} = \frac{p_{j|i} + p_{i|j}}{2N}$$ $$q_{ij} = \frac{(1 + \|\mathbf{y}_i - \mathbf{y}_j\|^2)^{-1}}{\sum_{k \neq l} (1 + \|\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{y}_l\|^2)^{-1}}, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} = \sum_{i \neq j} p_{ij} \log \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}$$

E. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP)

$$p_{i|j} = \exp\left(-\frac{\max(0, d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) - \rho_i)}{\sigma_i}\right), \quad p_{ij} = p_{i|j} + p_{j|i} - p_{i|j} p_{j|i}$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = \sum_{i \neq j} \left[ p_{ij} \log \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}} + (1 - p_{ij}) \log \frac{1 - p_{ij}}{1 - q_{ij}} \right]$$

9. Experimental Procedure

  1. Data Retrieval: Loaded wdbc.data from the local lab repository and validated complete receipt of 569 instances.
  2. Parsing & Extraction: Isolated the identification ID and Diagnosis target vector ($y \in \{M, B\}$) from the 30 continuous numerical feature columns.
  3. Standardization: Applied column-wise z-score transformation ($z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$) to eliminate dimensional scale bias.
  4. PCA Computation: Formed the $30 \times 30$ sample covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ and solved the eigensystem via cyclic Jacobi rotation. Calculated explained variance ratios and projected onto the top 2 and 3 eigenvectors.
  5. SVD Computation: Evaluated the Gram matrix $\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X}$, derived singular values $\sigma_i$, and constructed the truncated low-rank projection.
  6. t-SNE Optimization: Parameterized perplexity ($\text{Perp} = 30$) and gradient descent iterations ($250$) to minimize Kullback-Leibler divergence.
  7. UMAP Optimization: Configured local neighborhood size ($k=15$) and minimum distance ($0.1$) to optimize fuzzy simplicial set cross-entropy.
  8. Visualization & Evaluation: Rendered 2D and 3D interactive Plotly scatter plots, generated scree variance plots, and recorded execution metrics into LocalStorage.

12. Comparative Analysis & Discussion

Based on the experimental observations across all four dimensionality reduction algorithms on the Wisconsin dataset, the following critical engineering and mathematical insights emerge:

  1. Global Variance vs. Local Manifold Preservation: PCA and SVD focus strictly on preserving directions of maximum global variance across all 30 features. While they successfully capture 63.18% of information in 2 components, they preserve global linear distances at the expense of local neighborhood resolution, resulting in a slight overlap boundary between benign and malignant cases. In contrast, t-SNE and UMAP prioritize local neighborhood affinities, successfully resolving this overlap into distinct, isolated tumor clusters.
  2. Computational Efficiency: PCA and SVD demonstrated near-instantaneous execution times (≤ 20 ms) due to deterministic closed-form eigendecomposition of the $30 \times 30$ covariance matrix. In contrast, t-SNE and UMAP require iterative stochastic optimization across all pairwise distances, requiring 200 ms to 1600 ms depending on sample size and iterations.
  3. Manifold Topology & Distance Interpretation: While t-SNE provides exceptional visual cluster separation, inter-cluster distances and cluster sizes in t-SNE space are arbitrary and do not reflect true Euclidean separations in $\mathbb{R}^{30}$. UMAP offers a superior compromise by maintaining both local clustering and broader global topological layout.

13. Conclusion

The experiment successfully implemented, analyzed, and comparatively evaluated PCA, SVD, t-SNE, and UMAP on the 30-dimensional Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer dataset. The study verified that PCA and SVD are optimal for fast, deterministic, linear variance compression (capturing over 63% of diagnostic variance in 2 components), while t-SNE and UMAP excel at non-linear exploratory visualization and clinical cluster separation. All empirical results matched theoretical expectations, validating the practical necessity of matching dimensionality reduction algorithms to specific analytical requirements in machine learning workflows.
